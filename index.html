Mathematical Theory & 3GPP TS 23.032 / SIB19 Specifications
STAGE 1 Curved Earth vs Flat Map: The Geodesic Dilemma

In terrestrial networks, cells are small (~1 km) and can be approximated as flat 2D planes. In Non-Terrestrial Networks (NTN), satellite beam footprints span hundreds of kilometers across Earth's spherical surface.

The shortest path between two points on Earth is a geodesic curve (great circle), not a straight line on a flat Mercator or equirectangular projection. Naive planar ray-casting directly on latitude/longitude coordinates produces catastrophic errors of up to 150–200 km at high latitudes and across wide beams!

Geodesic Arc via Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP):
v(t) = [sin((1-t)θ) / sin(θ)] · v₁ + [sin(tθ) / sin(θ)] · v₂
where θ = arccos(v₁ · v₂) on the unit sphere
STAGE 2 Tier-1 Minimum Bounding Rectangle (MBR) Fast-Reject & 180° Date Line Split

Before executing trigonometric projections, the UE performs an $O(1)$ bounding box check. If the UE is outside the latitude/longitude envelope $[\phi_{min}, \phi_{max}] \times [\lambda_{min}, \lambda_{max}]$, it is instantly rejected.

The 180° Antimeridian Challenge: When a cell spans the International Date Line (e.g. Fiji, Tonga, Kiribati), longitudes jump from $+180^\circ$ to $-180^\circ$. A standard min/max would span the entire globe! Our engine detects this discontinuity and partitions the envelope into dual intervals:

Antimeridian Longitude Envelope Condition:
λ_ue ∈ [λ_min_east, +180°] ∪ [-180°, λ_max_west]
STAGE 3 Gnomonic Tangent Projection: The Great Circle Miracle

The Gnomonic projection projects points on a sphere from Earth's center onto a local plane tangent to the cell Centroid $(\phi_0, \lambda_0)$.

The Fundamental Mathematical Property: Because every great circle lies in a plane passing through Earth's center, its projection onto the tangent plane is the intersection of two planes — which is an exact straight line segment! Therefore, geodesic polygon boundaries on Earth become mathematically exact flat polygon edges on the tangent plane.

cos(c) = sin(φ₀)·sin(φ) + cos(φ₀)·cos(φ)·cos(λ - λ₀)
x = [cos(φ)·sin(λ - λ₀)] / cos(c)
y = [cos(φ₀)·sin(φ) - sin(φ₀)·cos(φ)·cos(λ - λ₀)] / cos(c)
Valid for the visible hemisphere where cos(c) > 0 (c < 90°)
STAGE 4 Jordan Curve Ray-Casting & The 3GPP TS 23.032 Right-Hand Rule Trap

Once mapped to the tangent plane, the UE location $(x_0, y_0)$ is evaluated using the Jordan Curve Parity Theorem. A horizontal ray is cast in the $+X$ direction:

Parity Rule: Intersections mod 2 == 1 ⟹ INSIDE | Intersections mod 2 == 0 ⟹ OUTSIDE

🪤 The TS 23.032 Clause 5.3 Trap: 3GPP explicitly mandates that polygon vertices must be ordered clockwise. The interior is defined as the region to the right of the perimeter traversal. If a network operator inadvertently broadcasts vertices in counter-clockwise order, the standard defines the interior as the entire rest of the planet! The simulator's live winding toggle demonstrates this exact vulnerability.
